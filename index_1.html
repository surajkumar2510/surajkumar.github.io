About

Robotics researcher working across marine vehicle dynamics, autonomous driving perception, and applied machine learning.

I am an MS research scholar in Robotics and Automation at the Department of Engineering Design, IIT Madras, working in the Autonomous Systems Lab under Dr. Asokan Thondiyath. My thesis studies the dynamics and control of a 123 kg buoyancy-driven winged underwater glider — building the vehicle's complete mathematical model from CFD-extracted hydrodynamic coefficients, through a six-degree-of-freedom Fossen simulator, to the control design that flies it in sawtooth glides and controlled spirals.

In parallel I work on perception for autonomous driving: multimodal sensor fusion across camera, LiDAR, IMU and GPS; detect–classify–track pipelines; trajectory prediction for on-road traffic; and production ADAS features delivered to a leading Japanese automotive client.

Before returning to research I spent three and a half years building real systems — autonomous underwater vehicles and an underwater docking station at CSIR-CMERI Durgapur, UAV landing-gear hydraulics under a DRDO programme at IIT Madras, and wire-arc additive manufacturing research at CSIR-NML Jamshedpur. That mix is what I bring to a problem: a mechanical engineer's instinct for the physical system, plus the simulation, control and learning tooling to reason about it rigorously.

MS thesis — underwater glider dynamics and control

Dynamics and Control of a Buoyancy-Driven Winged Underwater Glider

MS Thesis · Underwater Robotics

2025 — Present

Underwater gliders convert small, slow changes in buoyancy into forward motion by trading vertical velocity for horizontal glide across a fixed wing. They carry no propeller and consume almost no power between buoyancy cycles, which makes them the natural platform for month-scale ocean survey. The engineering difficulty is that the vehicle is under-actuated: a single bellows and a rudder must produce the full six-degree-of-freedom trajectory. My thesis builds the complete mathematical model of a 123 kg winged glider — 2.4 m hull, 0.6 m maximum diameter — and the control design that flies it. Because no experimental facility is available, every hydrodynamic coefficient is obtained by virtual testing in CFD, which makes the verification of that CFD a first-class part of the work rather than an afterthought.

ANSYS FluentMATLAB/SimulinkFossen 6-DOFSNAME / NEDUnder-actuated control

Added-mass identification

  • Complete 6-DOF added-mass matrix extracted through forced-oscillation simulations using an overset-mesh setup in ANSYS Fluent.
  • Validated against Lamb's analytical prolate-spheroid theory before adoption into the dynamics model.
  • Cross-checked against DARPA SUBOFF AFF-1 pure-sway planar-motion-mechanism runs.

Damping matrix extraction

  • Full bare-hull damping matrix — all six diagonal terms plus pitch–heave and yaw–sway cross-terms — extracted from CFD.
  • Rotational damping in roll, pitch and yaw obtained with a rotation-invariant cylindrical overset component and a coupled solver, after diagnosing orphan-cell and divergence failures.
  • Identified and corrected a drag double-counting error between the wing lookup tables and the hull damping block by re-running the whole campaign on the bare hull.

Mesh verification

  • Formal three-mesh grid convergence study — 1.61 M, 2.94 M and 6.69 M cells — with GCI below 1% on the fine mesh; the medium mesh adopted for production runs.
  • ITTC-compliant domain sizing with blockage held under 0.6%, area-averaged y⁺ ≈ 0.57, k-ω SST closure.
  • Benchmark validation against the published ALEX glider before applying the method to the in-house geometry.

Virtual captive-model testing

  • Implemented the David Taylor Model Basin captive-test suite virtually in Fluent: resistance, straight-line oblique towing and rotating-arm tests.
  • Showed that pure crossflow tests capture the quadratic damping term but miss the linear body-lift term — a finding that changed the coefficient set used in the simulator.
  • Separated the Munk moment from viscous yaw damping analytically and confirmed the split against two independent test methods.

Six-degree-of-freedom simulator

  • Built a complete Fossen-formulation 6-DOF simulator in MATLAB/Simulink from scratch, in the SNAME/NED body frame, with modular subsystems for buoyancy, restoring forces, damping, Coriolis (rigid-body and added-mass), mass-matrix inversion, wing hydrodynamics, rudder and the Euler-angle Jacobian.
  • Wing module driven by CFD-derived CL/CD lookup tables across an angle-of-attack sweep; buoyancy engine modelled as a single metallic bellows with cosine S-curve ramping and CAD-derived centre-of-gravity and centre-of-buoyancy coordinates.
  • Diagnosed and corrected a coordinate-datum frame mismatch that was the root cause of persistent simulation failure, along with quadratic-damping sign errors, Jacobian gimbal-lock singularities and algebraic loops.
  • Achieves stable sawtooth glide: pitch settling at ±25°, glide speed ≈ 0.33 m/s, ≈ 65 m depth excursion per leg.
  • Added a ventral rudder — modelled with the Whicker–Fehlner lift slope, finite-aspect-ratio correction and a stall limit — giving the vehicle yaw authority it otherwise lacks. Result: a steady spiral of 7.5 revolutions in 1580 s at roughly 10 m turn radius.
